# Part 107 Ground School

## What is in it

- **386 questions.** 308 written training items with every answer choice explained and linked to the exact rule, 32 held-out validation items that never appear in practice, and the FAA's own 46 sample questions kept in their own section. 28 questions show the real FAA testing-supplement figures on screen, the way the test now does.
- **15 generators** that produce fresh numeric and chart problems every time (altitude limits, cloud clearance, AGL/MSL conversion, METAR decoding, obstruction heights, sector math, currency dates, loading) so memorizing an item teaches nothing.
- **Readiness model.** A Beta posterior per exam area with evidence decay, 4,000 Monte Carlo draws against the real 60-question blueprint, and an 11-check gate that is deliberately harder than the 70% pass mark. It prints a probability, not a feeling.
- **Learn.** Ten modules built from the primary sources, each with a hands-on lesson inside: airspace placement, name-the-line and symbol hunt, a METAR generator, a Mode 2 transmitter that drives the drone drawing, and a go / not yet / no-go job card.
- **Lab.** Nine tap-to-explore diagrams, a Chart Lab with the real FAA-CT-8080-2H figures and legend, a Weather Lab driven by sliders, drone anatomy with pitch/roll/yaw, an airport whose runway and pattern follow the wind, and a chart symbol challenge.
- **Mission 107.** Five branching simulations (flight authorization with a LAANC request, weather go/no-go with a TAF, night operation, operations over people and Remote ID, emergency and reporting) with timed events and a debrief that links every decision to its section.
- **Practice.** Area drills, the most-missed-code drill (UA.I.E.K13, Declaration of Compliance), misconceptions, spaced repetition, cram set, image-based questions, flashcards, bookmarks and a notebook of misses.
- **Exams.** Full and challenge simulations, optional 120-minute timer, the FAA official 46, and the validation exam.
- **More.** Date Check (every rule change with its source), knowledge map, achievements, backup codes to move progress between devices, a place to record your real score, coverage audit, reference, and About.

## Sources

Everything is written against the current text of 14 CFR parts 107, 89 and 48, the AIM, the Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge and the Aeronautical Chart User's Guide, then independently re-checked against those sources. The citation and a deep link are shown on every question. The exam blueprint (Regulations 48%, Operations 25%, Airspace 20%, Weather 5%, Loading and performance 2%) comes from the PSI UAG Applicant Information Bulletin effective 29 September 2025, which the FAA's August 2025 Airman Testing Community Advisory names as the current blueprint. Chart and weather figures are pages from FAA-CT-8080-2H, a US Government work in the public domain. The FAA publishes no answer key for its sample questions; those answers are ours and cited.
